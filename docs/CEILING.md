# The ceiling

Generated by `scripts/render-ceiling.py` from `data/gap/`. Do not edit by hand.

Set D is 1,000 factors. 185 are covered at L2 or better, so the gap is 815. This document classifies that gap, because a gap you cannot act on is just a number that makes you feel bad.

## The three classes

| class | meaning | cost | factors |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | derivable with noise. The mechanism is published, so it can be written from what exists. The error is the author's misreading. | authoring | **198** |
| II | inferable from structure. A property of a running system, visible only under a probe. | a lab and a target | **567** |
| III | genuinely unavailable. A fact about one organisation's internals, or unpublished research. No defensible proxy. | impossible from outside | **50** |

**The ceiling is 95.0 percent.** That is the share of Set D any collection could reach from outside, given unlimited authoring and lab time. It is not a forecast of what this repo will reach, and it is not a promise.

The ceiling is high, and that is the most important finding in this document. Almost nothing in the denominator is hidden. The constraint on completeness here is effort and access to a lab, not secrecy. Fifty one factors are permanently out of reach and most of them are about how a particular company runs itself, which no external party can know.

Read it by column, not by row.

- **Class I is 198 factors.** This is the cheapest coverage in the whole project: no target, no lab, no permission. Write the entry with a procedure and an oracle and it moves. L13 promises the most here, because statute and standards are published and this repo simply has not written them down.
- **Class II is 567 factors, and it is the real project.** Nearly every lens is dominated by it. It is also where the current zero lives: nothing is observed because no lab exists, and two thirds of the gap can only be closed with one.
- **Class III is 50 factors.** Seventeen of them are organisational. The honest statement is that a public repo cannot answer them, and any collection claiming to has invented an answer.

## What the ceiling means for the claim

Three statements are now defensible, and they are ranked by strength.

1. **This repo covers 18.5 percent of a published denominator of 1,000 factors.** Checkable by anyone who reads `data/denominator/` and `data/coverage/coverage.jsonl` and disagrees with a grade.
2. **18.5 percent today, 38.3 percent if every Class I factor were authored, 95.0 percent at the ceiling.** The middle number is the one an author can reach alone.
3. **A public collection cannot exceed the ceiling**, because 50 factors depend on facts no outsider can obtain.

What is not defensible, and will not be written in the README: that this is complete, that it covers everything, or that the number is high. It is a fraction with a denominator, and the denominator is the contribution.
